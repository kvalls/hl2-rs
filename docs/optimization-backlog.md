# Optimization backlog

Goal (owner, 2026-11-21): maxed settings at native Retina (3456x2168, 4x MSAA, full HDR, motion
blur, anisotropic 8x, VSync) on an Apple M1 Pro at a steady 120 FPS: the slowest 1% of frames under
8.33 ms. Method: capture, add findings here, fix the largest or most frequent cost, re-capture the
same path. Tooling and capture layers: docs/DESIGN.md "Profiling toward a steady 120 FPS";
measurements: docs/performance.md.

Each item: evidence (capture, numbers), suspected cause, next step. Status: open, measuring,
fixing, done (with the commit and the before/after numbers), rejected (with the reason).
Numbers from trace builds are inflated by the trace itself; compare them only with each other.

## Measuring an item (owner, 2026-11-22)

Before an item is fixed, decide how its gain will show and record it in its Measure column: no
single benchmark covers every area, and a change can be worthwhile where the crowd fight barely
moves. Pick the scene that stresses the area the item touches and the measure that area moves:

- What it changes: CPU stage (`--profile` stage mean, p95, p99 bound), GPU pass time (headless
  Metal System Trace per pass), frame pacing (displayed-surface intervals, missed refreshes), hitch
  frequency and size (the slowest frames, not the mean), load time, memory peak, or allocations.
- Where it shows: a cost the crowd lacks needs its own scene. When none exists, add one under
  `test-inputs/performance-research/` (or a frame-matched replay capture for fidelity).
- Which bound limits the frame there: a CPU cut in a GPU-bound scene (the static spawn at maxed
  settings) shows as render-thread wait, not frame time; say so and measure the stage itself.
- Repeats and noise: alternate the builds, at least three runs per side, and compare against two
  runs of one build when a scene is not frame-deterministic.
